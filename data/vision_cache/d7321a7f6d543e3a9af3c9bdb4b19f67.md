*A pictograph represents data through pictures of objects. It helps answer questions about data with just a quick glance.*

In the above pictograph, one unit or symbol (☺) is used to represent one student. There are also other pictographs where one unit or symbol stands for many people or objects.

### Example

**Example:** Nand Kishor collected responses from the children of his middle school in Berasia regarding how often they slept at least 9 hours during the night. He prepared a pictograph from the data:

| Response | Number of Children ([Figure: green triangle symbol] = 10 Children) |
|---|---|
| Always | [Figure: 5 green triangles] |
| Sometimes | [Figure: 2 full green triangles and 1 half triangle] |
| Never | [Figure: 4 green triangles] |

**Answer the following questions using the pictograph:**

1. What is the number of children who always slept at least 9 hours at night?
2. How many children sometimes slept at least 9 hours at night?
3. How many children always slept less than 9 hours each night? Explain how you got your answer.

*Solutions*

1. In the table, there are 5 pictures [Figure: green triangle] for 'Always'. Each picture [Figure: green triangle] represents 10 children. Therefore, 5 pictures indicate $5 \times 10 = 50$ children.
2. There are 2 complete pictures [Figure: green triangle] ($2 \times 10 = 20$) and a half picture [Figure: half green triangle] (half of $10 = 5$). Therefore, the number of children who sleep at least 9 hours only sometimes is $20 + 5 = 25$.
