2. **Use the points on the circles and/or their centres to form isosceles and equilateral triangles. The circles are of the same size.**

[Figure: Two overlapping circles of the same size with centres A and B, and A and B joined by a line. The circles intersect at points C (top) and D (bottom). Points C₁, C₂, C₃ lie on the circle with centre B, and points D₁, D₂, D₃ lie on the circle with centre A, along the upper arcs, labelled D₃, D₂, D₁, C, C₁, C₂, C₃. Dashed lines join A to C₁, C₂, C₃ and B to C₁, C₂, C₃. Triangles ABC and ABD are drawn with solid lines.]

[Figure: Three overlapping circles of the same size. Points A and B lie where the circles intersect near the middle, C is at the top intersection and D is at the bottom intersection.]

**Ans:**
Take points C, C₁, C₂, C₃ … on the circle with centre B. The triangles ABC, AC₁B, AC₂B, … are isosceles triangle. Similarly for points D₁, D₂, D₃ … ΔADB, ΔAD₁B, ΔAD₂B, … are isosceles triangles.
ΔABC and ΔADB are equilateral triangle.
Similarly it can be done for the other figure.
