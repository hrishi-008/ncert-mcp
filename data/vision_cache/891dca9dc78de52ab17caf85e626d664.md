### Example 2: Which is greater? $1023 + 125$ *or* $1022 + 128$?

Imagining a situation could help us answer this without finding the values. Raja had 1023 marbles and got 125 more today. Now he has $1023 + 125$ marbles. Joy had 1022 marbles and got 128 more today. Now he has $1022 + 128$ marbles. Who has more?

> [Figure: Raja (1023 + 125): a bar labelled 1022 followed by a single circle labelled 1, and below it a shorter bar labelled 125. Joy (1022 + 128): a bar labelled 1022, and below it a bar labelled 125 followed by three circles each labelled 1.]

This situation can be represented as shown in the picture on the right. To begin with, Raja had 1 more marble than Joy. But Joy got 3 more marbles than Raja today. We can see that Joy has (two) more marbles than Raja now.
That is,

$$1023 + 125 < 1022 + 128.$$

### Example 3: Which is greater? $113 - 25$ *or* $112 - 24$?

Imagine a situation, Raja had 113 marbles and lost 25 of them. He has $113 - 25$ marbles. Joy had 112 marbles and lost 24 today. He has $112 - 24$ marbles. Who has more marbles left with them?

> [Figure: Raja (113 – 25): a bar labelled 112 followed by a single circle labelled 1, with a brace beneath the end marked "24" with arrows and the word "remove". Joy (112 – 24): a bar labelled 112, with a brace beneath its end marked "24" with arrows and the word "remove".]

Raja had 1 marble more than Joy. But he also lost 1 marble more than Joy did. Therefore, they have an equal number of marbles now.
That is,

$$113 - 25 = 112 - 24.$$
