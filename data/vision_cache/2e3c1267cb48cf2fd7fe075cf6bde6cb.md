## 2.5 Angle

An angle is formed by two rays having a common starting point. Here is an angle formed by rays $\overrightarrow{BD}$ and $\overrightarrow{BE}$ where B is the common starting point (Fig. 2.8).

> [Figure: Fig. 2.8 — two rays starting at the common point B, labelled "vertex", going to points D (upper right) and E (lower right) with arrowheads; each ray is labelled "arm".]

The point B is called the **vertex** of the angle, and the rays $\overrightarrow{BD}$ and $\overrightarrow{BE}$ are called the **arms** of the angle. How can we name this angle? We can simply use the vertex and say that it is the Angle B. To be clearer, we use a point on each of the arms together with the vertex to name the angle. In this case, we name the angle as Angle DBE or Angle EBD. The word angle can be replaced by the symbol '$\angle$', i.e., $\angle$DBE or $\angle$EBD. Note that in specifying the angle, the vertex is always written as the middle letter.

To indicate an angle, we use a small curve at the vertex (refer to Fig. 2.9).
