### Order of Multiplication

We know that $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8}$.

[Figure: A grid of 2 columns and 4 rows. The top row is outlined in bold; its left cell is hatched with blue diagonal lines and yellow, labelled $\frac{1}{8}$, and its right cell is solid yellow. A bracket above the columns is labelled $\frac{1}{2}$ and a bracket on the left of the top row is labelled $\frac{1}{4}$.]

[Figure: A grid of 4 columns and 2 rows. The left column is outlined in bold; its top cell is hatched with blue diagonal lines and yellow, labelled $\frac{1}{8}$, and its bottom cell is solid yellow. A bracket above the first column is labelled $\frac{1}{4}$ and a bracket on the left of the first column is labelled $\frac{1}{2}$.]

Now, what is $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{2}$?

That is $\frac{1}{8}$ too.

In general, note that the area of a rectangle remains the same even if the length and breadth are interchanged.

The order of multiplication does not matter. Thus,

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{c}{d} \times \frac{a}{b}.$$

This can also be seen from Brahmagupta's formula for multiplying fractions.
