Thus, the procedure of division using place value can be extended to find quotients with decimal values. Ones can be regrouped as tenths, tenths can be regrouped as hundredths and so on.

**Example 9 :** Find the value of $237 \div 8$.

$237 \div 8 \longrightarrow$ Divide 2 Hundreds + 3 Tens + 7 Ones into 8 equal parts.

To divide 2 Hundred into 8 equal parts we need to regrouped them as 20 Tens.

20 Tens + 3 Tens = 23 Tens.

$23$ Tens $\div 8 \longrightarrow$ 2 Tens, and 7 Tens remain.

> [Figure: Long division with columns H, T, O. Shows 8) 237 (0 2, with –16 and remainder 7 below; an arrow points from the subtraction line to "Tens".]

7 Tens can be regrouped as 70 Ones.

70 Ones + 7 Ones = 77 Ones.

$77$ Ones $\div 8 \longrightarrow$ 9 Ones, and 5 Ones remain.

> [Figure: Long division with columns H, T, O. Shows 8) 237 (0 2 9, with –16, 77, –72, 5 below; arrows point to "Tens" (at –16 line) and "Ones" (at 5).]

To divide 5 Ones into 8 equal parts we need to regroup them as 50 Tenths.

**When we regroup Ones into Tenths, we place a decimal point in the quotient.**

50 Tenths $\div 8 \longrightarrow$ 6 Tenths, and 2 Tenths remain.

[Figure: Long division with columns H, T, O and Tenths. Shows 8) 237 (0 2 9 . 6, with 16, 77, 72, 50, 48, 2 below; arrows point to "Tens", "Ones" and "Tenths".]

> [Figure: A girl with a hand on her chin, thinking, with a speech bubble: "Remember, when we regroup Ones into Tenths we need to place a decimal point in the quotient."]

2 Tenths cannot be divided into 8 equal parts. So we need to regroup them as 20 Hundredths.

20 Hundredths $\div 8 \longrightarrow$ 2 Hundredths, and 4 Hundredths remain.
