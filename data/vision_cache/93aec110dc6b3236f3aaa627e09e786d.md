We have,

$$AB = AC \text{ (given)}$$
$$\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 90° \text{ (from construction)}$$

AD is a common side of the two triangles $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle ADC$.

[Figure: Isosceles triangle ABC with apex A at the top, base BC, and D on BC. AD is drawn from A perpendicular to BC, with a right-angle mark at D. Sides AB and AC each carry a single tick mark. Labels: A, B, C, D.]

Thus, the triangles satisfy the RHS condition. Hence, $\triangle ADB \cong \triangle ADC$.

This shows that $\angle B = \angle C$, as they are corresponding parts of congruent triangles.

Thus, the angles opposite to equal sides are equal.
