### Let's Explore!

Why is area generally measured using squares?

> [Figure: A girl with a puzzled expression and a speech bubble saying "Why can't we use circles instead of squares to find the area?"]

Draw a circle on a graph sheet with diameter (breadth) of length 3. Count the squares and use them to estimate the area of the circular region.

As you can see, circles can't be packed tightly without gaps in between. So, it is difficult to get an accurate measurement of area using circles as units. Here, the same rectangle is packed in two different ways with circles—the first one has 42 circles and the second one has 44 circles.

[Figure: Two identical rectangles packed with circles. The first has circles arranged in a regular grid of rows and columns (42 circles); the second has circles arranged in staggered rows (44 circles). Gaps are visible between the circles in both.]

Try using different shapes (triangle and rectangle) to fill the given space (without overlaps and gaps) and find out the merits associated with using a square shape to find the area rather than another shape. List out the points that make a square the best shape to use to measure area.
