# 7 A TALE OF THREE INTERSECTING LINES

A triangle is the most basic closed shape. As we know, it consists of:

- three corner points, that we call the vertex of the triangle, and
- three line segments or the sides of the triangle that join the pairs of vertices.

Triangles come in various shapes. Some of them are shown below.

[Figure: Four triangles. The first is $\Delta$ ABC with vertices A (top), B (bottom left), C (bottom right). The second is $\Delta$ YZX with X (top), Y (bottom left), Z (bottom right). The third is $\Delta$WUV with U (top), V (bottom left), W (bottom right). The fourth is $\Delta$BAU with B (top), A (left), U (bottom right).]

Observe the symbol used to denote a triangle and how the triangles are named using their vertices. While naming a triangle, the vertices can come in any order.

The three sides meeting at the corners give rise to three angles that we call the angles of the triangle. For example, in $\Delta$ABC, these angles are $\angle$CAB, $\angle$ABC, $\angle$BCA, which we simply denote as $\angle$A, $\angle$B and $\angle$C, respectively.
