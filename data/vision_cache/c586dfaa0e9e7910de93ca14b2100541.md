Example 12: Ramesh and Suresh have 60 marbles between them. Ramesh has 30 more marbles than Suresh. How many marbles does each boy have?

> [Figure: Two boys in light blue shirts and blue trousers crouching on the ground, playing with marbles scattered in front of them.]

In this problem, we have two unknowns. If we denote the number of marbles with Ramesh as $x$ and the number of marbles with Suresh as $y$, then what are the different equations that we have?

1. The total number of marbles is 60.
$$x + y = 60.$$

2. Ramesh has 30 marbles more than Suresh.
$$x = y + 30.$$

[Figure: Two boxes of red dots. The box labelled "Suresh" has two rows of red dots, each row shown as three dots, then ". . . . . . .", then a final dot. The box labelled "Ramesh" has the same two rows of red dots, with a dashed outline around it that also encloses a row of circled numbers ① ② ③ . . . . . . . ㉚ below the dots, showing 30 extra marbles.]

How do we find the unknowns using these equations? So far, we have only developed a strategy to solve an equation with one unknown!
To get such an equation, we can do the following.

Denote the number of marbles with Suresh as $y$, and the number of marbles with Ramesh as $y + 30$.
Since the total number of marbles is 60, we have the equation:
$$y + (y + 30) = 60$$
$$2y + 30 = 60.$$

Use this to find both the unknowns.
