Here are some examples of how algebraic expressions in modern notation were written in ancient Indian notation:

| Modern Notation | Ancient Indian Notation |
|---|---|
| $2x + 1$ | *yā 2 rū 1* (in each term the indication of unknown/known came first) |
| $2x - 8$ | *yā 2 rū* $\dot{8}$ (a dot over the number indicated that it was negative) |
| $3x + 4 = 2x + 8$ | *yā 3 rū 4* <br> *yā 2 rū 8* (the two sides of an equation were presented one below the other) |

### Example 16

**Example 16:** *Bījagaṇita* by Bhāskarāchārya (1150 CE) mentions this problem.

One man has ₹300 rupees and 6 horses. Another man has 10 horses and a debt of ₹100. If they are equally rich and the price of each horse is the same, tell me the price of one horse.

**Solution:**

Let price of one horse = ₹ $x$

According to the problem

$$300 + 6x = 10x - 100$$

$$300 + 6x + (100) = 10x$$

$$400 + 6x = 10x$$

$$400 = 10x - 6x$$

$$400 = 4x$$

$$400 \div 4 = x$$

$$100 = x$$

Therefore, the price of one horse = ₹ 100.
