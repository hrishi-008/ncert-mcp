Can it be done by changing the angles between the dotted lines?

If a figure with three radial arms should have rotational symmetry, then a rotated version of it should overlap with the original. Here are rough diagrams of both of them.

If these two figures must overlap, what can you tell about the angles?

[Figure: Two three-armed radial figures side by side, each with one arm pointing up and two arms pointing down-left and down-right, with dotted lines along the middle of each arm. In the left figure the angles are labelled C (between the up arm and the down-left arm), A (between the up arm and the down-right arm) and B (between the two lower arms). In the right figure the labels are B (between the up arm and the down-left arm), C (between the up arm and the down-right arm) and A (between the two lower arms).]

Observe that $\angle A$ must overlap $\angle B$, $\angle B$ must overlap $\angle C$ and $\angle C$ must overlap $\angle A$.

So, $\angle A = \angle B = \angle C$. What must this angle be?

We know that a full turn has 360 degrees. This is equally distributed amongst these three angles. So each angle must be $\frac{360°}{3} = 120°$.

So, the radial arms figure with 3 arms shows rotational symmetry when the angle between the adjacent dotted lines is $120°$. Use paper cutouts to verify this observation.
