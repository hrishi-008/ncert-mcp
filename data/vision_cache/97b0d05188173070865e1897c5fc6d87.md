## 6.2 Picking Parity

Kishor has some number cards and is working on a puzzle: There are 5 boxes, and each box should contain exactly 1 number card. The numbers in the boxes should sum to 30. Can you help him find a way to do it?

[Figure: Five empty boxes in the equation $\square + \square + \square + \square + \square = 30$]

[Figure: Collections of number cards: three purple cards showing 13, four pink cards showing 9, three green cards showing 1, four yellow cards showing 7, three light green cards showing 11, four orange cards showing 5, four blue cards showing 3]

Can you figure out which 5 cards add to 30? Is it possible?
There are many ways of choosing 5 cards from this collection.
Is there a way to find a solution without checking all possibilities?
Let us find out.

> **?** Add a few even numbers together. What kind of number do you get? Does it matter how many numbers are added?

Any even number can be arranged in pairs without any leftovers. Some even numbers are shown here, arranged in pairs.

[Figure: Three groups of pink dots arranged in pairs: a group of 6 dots in 3 rows of 2, a group of 10 dots arranged diagonally in pairs, and a group of 20 dots in 2 rows of 10]

> [Figure: Five pairs of dots in 5 rows of 2, the top three green and the bottom two purple]

As we see in the figure, adding any number of even numbers will result in a number which can still be arranged in pairs without any leftovers. In other words, the sum will always be an even number.

> **?** Now, add a few odd numbers together. What kind of number do you get? Does it matter how many odd numbers are added?

Odd numbers can not be arranged in pairs. An odd number is one more than a collection of pairs. Some odd numbers are shown below:
