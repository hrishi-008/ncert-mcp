Take a cardboard and make an angle-shaped slit as shown below by tracing and cutting out the shape of one of the rotating arms.

[Figure: A boy peeks over a table at a cardboard piece with an angle-shaped slit. Above it float four pairs of rotating arms (blue and red) joined at a hinge, each pair with a different angle between the arms.]

Now, shuffle and mix up all the rotating arms. Can you identify which of the rotating arms will pass through the slit?

The correct one can be found by placing each of the rotating arms over the slit. Let us do this for some of the rotating arms:

[Figure: Three cardboard pieces with the angle-shaped slit, each with a pair of rotating arms placed over the slit. In the first, the red arm lies inside the slit angle, closer to the blue arm. In the second, the red arm sticks out beyond the slit. In the third, both arms fit exactly along the slit.]

> Slit angle is greater than the arms' angle. The arms will not go through the slit.

> Slit angle is less than the arms' angle. The arms will not go through the slit.

> Slit angle is equal to the arms' angle. The arms will go through the slit.

Only the pair of rotating arms where the angle is equal to that of the slit passes through the slit. Note that the possibility of passing through the slit depends only on the angle between the rotating arms and not on their lengths (as long as they are shorter than the length of the slit).
