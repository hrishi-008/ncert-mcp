### Figures with more than one line of symmetry

Does a square have only one line of symmetry?
Take a square piece of paper. By folding, find all its lines of symmetry.

[Figure: A sequence of paper-folding steps. A plain square sheet, then "Fold 1" showing the square folded in half vertically into a tall rectangle, then "Fold 2" showing it folded in half horizontally into a flat rectangle, then "Fold 3" showing a right-angled triangle formed by a diagonal fold, then "Fold 4" showing a triangle folded along the other diagonal, and finally an opened square showing the crease lines: vertical, horizontal and both diagonals.]

Here are the different folds giving different lines of symmetry.

- Fold the paper into half vertically.
- Fold it again into half horizontally (i.e., you have folded it twice). Now open out the folds.

[Figure: Two folded sheets. "Vertical Fold" shows a paper folded vertically with a curved arrow pointing to the right on the left side. "Horizontal Fold" shows a paper folded horizontally with a curved arrow pointing upward at the bottom.]

Again fold the square into half (for a third time now), but this time along a diagonal, as shown in the figure. Again, open it.
